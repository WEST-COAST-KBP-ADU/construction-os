## Question

Who competes for ADU customers in the Sacramento region, how are their sites
structured, what makes homeowners trust and choose a builder, and what does
that imply for westcoastkbp.com?

## What makes homeowners choose a builder (converging trust factors)

1. **CSLB license** verifiable, matching business name, plus insurance/bond.
2. **Local track record** — completed ADUs in the same city; local permit
   experience.
3. **Reviews** in volume (top local competitors have 50–157).
4. **Price transparency** — fixed-price or fully itemized proposals.
5. **Warranty** — 1–2 years workmanship typical.
6. **Clear process** — named steps from consultation to keys.

## Standard site architecture of winning competitors

1. ADU type pages: detached / attached / garage conversion / JADU
2. Floor plans / models (sq ft, often prices)
3. Cost/pricing transparency page + per-city cost guides (dominant SEO play)
4. Financing page
5. Process page (numbered steps)
6. Portfolio / gallery / case studies
7. Reviews / testimonials
8. **Per-city service-area pages** (the main local-SEO lever)
9. FAQ
10. Blog / guides engine
11. Credentials block: CSLB number, insured, warranty
12. Consultation CTA; calculators/configurators at the top end

## Gap analysis: westcoastkbp.com today

Current site is a single-page platform preview: no service pages, no city
pages, no portfolio, no reviews, no cost/financing content, deliberately inert
CTAs. That was correct for the preview stage; for lead generation it competes
with none of the structures above.

**Strategic fit:** the platform's own positioning — visible project control,
owner-reviewed commitments — matches trust factor #4/#6 better than any
competitor's generic "full service" claim. This is the differentiator to build
the site around.

## Tensions requiring an owner decision

1. **Cost content vs. our boundary.** Competitors win search with cost guides;
   our rules currently forbid public cost commitments. Options: (a) publish
   educational ranges clearly attributed to third-party/market data with
   no-commitment disclaimers; (b) keep zero numbers and differentiate on
   process transparency. This needs a decision record before content work.
2. **Proof assets need owner facts.** Portfolio, review strategy, CSLB number,
   project counts, warranty terms — these are documents/facts to collect from
   the business, not content I can research.

## Synthesis — recommended direction (not a decision)

Phased information architecture for owner review:

- **Phase 1 — structure and trust:** four service pages (ADU, garage
  conversion, residential GC, GC/subcontract), process page, credentials/about
  block, FAQ, and seven per-city pages (Roseville, Rocklin, Lincoln, Folsom,
  Granite Bay, El Dorado Hills, Citrus Heights).
- **Phase 2 — proof:** portfolio/case studies, reviews program, per-city
  educational guides (cost guides only after the cost-content decision).
- **Phase 3 — product experience:** floor plans/models, financing page,
  calculator/configurator — where the market is clearly heading.
