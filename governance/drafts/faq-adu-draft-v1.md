**Q: What is an ADU (accessory dwelling unit)?**
A: An ADU is a self-contained secondary home on the same lot as a primary
residence — a backyard cottage, a garage conversion, or an attached unit.
California law has made ADUs one of the most practical ways for homeowners to
add living space or rental potential.

**Q: Do I need a permit to build an ADU in the Sacramento region?**
A: ADU projects generally go through a city or county permitting process, and
requirements differ by jurisdiction. Whether and how the rules apply to a
specific property requires review by the local building authority. We structure
every project around visible permit and approval checkpoints rather than
assumptions.

**Q: Can an ADU fit on my lot?**
A: That depends on zoning, lot size, setbacks, and existing structures — and it
requires verification against official sources for your specific parcel. Our
platform approach treats feasibility as something to be reviewed and evidenced,
not promised up front.

**Q: How much does an ADU cost?**
A: Costs vary widely with size, site conditions, finishes, and jurisdiction
fees. We don't quote prices on the website or by phone; every estimate is
prepared and reviewed individually by the owner before anything is committed.

**Q: How long does an ADU project take?**
A: Timelines depend on design, permitting, and construction scope, and they
differ by jurisdiction. We don't publish or promise schedules; a realistic
timeline is part of the owner-reviewed project plan.

**Q: What is a garage conversion, and is it different from an ADU?**
A: A garage conversion turns existing garage space into living space. In many
California jurisdictions it is treated as a type of ADU with its own rules.
Which rules apply to your property requires official verification.

**Q: What areas does West Coast KBP serve?**
A: The Sacramento region: Roseville, Rocklin, Lincoln, Folsom, Granite Bay,
El Dorado Hills, Citrus Heights, and nearby areas.

**Q: How is West Coast KBP different from a typical contractor?**
A: We run construction as a controlled, visible process: every project is
organized around scope, state, approvals, evidence, and a clear next action —
with the owner personally reviewing every commitment before it's made.

**Q: Do you work with general contractors and subcontractors?**
A: Yes — GC and subcontract coordination is a first-class path, kept separate
from homeowner-facing intake.
